1. Understanding the Belgian B2B market

Belgium is a unique B2B market that is often underestimated by international sales teams. With over 1 million active companies and a highly educated business population, it offers significant opportunities for B2B prospecting.

  • SME-dominated economy: 99.8% of Belgian companies have fewer than 250 employees. Decision-makers are directly accessible.
  • High purchasing power: Belgium has one of the highest GDPs per capita in Europe.
  • International mindset: Belgian business professionals are typically multilingual and open to working with international partners.
  • Less saturated market: Belgian SMEs receive fewer cold emails than French or German counterparts — response rates are higher.

3. The language challenge in Belgium

Belgium has three official languages, and language choice is critical for prospecting success:

  • French: Wallonia (southern region) and parts of Brussels. Used by approximately 40% of the population.
  • Dutch (Flemish): Flanders (northern region) and parts of Brussels. Used by approximately 57% of the population.
  • German: small eastern region near the German border.
  • English: widely accepted in Brussels (EU bubble), tech companies, and international firms.

5. Finding decision-makers in Belgian companies

In Belgian SMEs, the decision-maker is typically the CEO, founder, or managing director. Here's how to identify them:

  • "About us" or "Team" page of the company website — most Belgian SMEs list their leadership there.
  • BCE (Banque Carrefour des Entreprises) — Belgium's official register lists legal company administrators.
  • LinkedIn — search by company name and job title.
  • AI analysis — DataCloser automatically extracts the decision-maker's name from the company's website content.

6. GDPR compliance in Belgium

Belgium enforces GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation), supervised by the APD/GBA (Autorité de Protection des Données / Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit).

What is allowed: contacting professionals at their professional email address with a message relevant to their business activity, provided you mention how you obtained their contact information and offer an opt-out mechanism.

7. Effective outreach strategy for Belgium

1

Match the language to the region

French for Wallonia and French-speaking Brussels companies, Dutch for Flemish companies. Sending in the wrong language significantly reduces response rates.

2

Personalize with a company-specific detail

Reference something specific from their website — a recent project, a service they offer, a market they serve. Generic emails perform 3x worse than personalized ones.

3

Optimal timing

Tuesday to Thursday, 9-11am or 2-4pm Belgian time. Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons. Belgian business culture values punctuality and respects office hours.

4

Plan 2-3 follow-ups

First contact + follow-up at D+5 + final follow-up at D+12. 70% of B2B sales in Belgium require at least 2 contact attempts before a response.
